Science Behind the Score

Key concepts behind drug–target binding affinity prediction.

01

What is a Drug?

A drug is a chemical substance that interacts with biological targets in the body to produce a biological effect. In AffiniX, a drug molecule is represented as a SMILES string, a text notation that describes its atoms and the bonds connecting them.

02

What is a Protein?

Proteins are biological molecules made of chains of amino acids. They perform essential functions in cells, and many act as targets for drugs. In AffiniX, a protein is represented by its amino acid sequence using one-letter codes.

03

What is Binding Affinity?

Binding affinity describes how strongly a drug molecule binds to a target protein. It reflects the tendency of the two molecules to associate under specified conditions. A stronger interaction generally corresponds to higher affinity.

04

How is Binding Affinity Estimated?

AffiniX uses the DeepDTA deep-learning model to estimate binding affinity from the drug's SMILES representation and the protein's amino acid sequence. The model learns patterns from experimental training data and predicts a numerical score: pKd for Davis or a KIBA score for KIBA. This is a computational estimate, not a laboratory measurement.
